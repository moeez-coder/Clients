## SECTION 3: HOW THEY WIN / CURRENT MESSAGING

- **Their pitch in their own words:**
  - "Of the people your organization hired over the past year, how many would you enthusiastically rehire?"
  - "Successful hiring isn't simply about filling positions. It's about bringing people into an organization who deliver measurable results, strengthen teams, and contribute to long-term business performance."
  - "We begin by understanding what a successful hire must accomplish, leadership capabilities, career motivations, and alignment with clearly defined business objectives rather than simply matching resumes against a job description."
  - "Many of the strongest candidates aren't actively looking for another position. They're successfully performing for another organization."
  - "We believe the true measure of recruiting success isn't how many resumes are presented or how quickly a vacancy is filled. It's whether the person hired delivers the results the business needs."

- **What makes them different:**
  1. Performance-Based Hiring Methodology: they define success criteria for the role first, then find people who have already demonstrated those outcomes. They develop position profiles outlining first-year expectations and measurables using SMART goals, then align candidates to those objectives.
  2. Single-candidate presentations: they present one candidate per role and that candidate gets hired. Eight (or nine) times in 2026, they filled a position by presenting a single candidate. This is the opposite of the "send a stack of resumes" approach.
  3. Passive talent access: direct-search approach targeting people who are not job hunting but are performing well elsewhere.
  4. Structured interviews and consistent candidate assessments: they help clients improve their own hiring process, not just fill the role.
  5. Deep industry expertise in manufacturing and fire & life safety, sustained over 15 years.
  6. 85% repeat business rate for 15 years. Their clients keep coming back.
  7. GSA Contract Holder for government recruiting.
  8. Network scale: part of GRN with 134+ offices and 500+ consultants worldwide, but they operate with a boutique, hands-on approach.

- **Proof points:**
  1. Over 90% presentation-to-interview ratio (candidates they present almost always get interviewed)
  2. Eight single-candidate hires in 2026 (nine referenced in the email copy)
  3. 85% repeat business for 15 years
  4. Fire and Life Safety Client case study: 380 candidates searched, 10 presented, 8 completed placements
  5. GSA Contract Holder
  6. Nearly 15 years in business (founded 2013)

- **Pain points they solve:**
  1. Cannot find qualified candidates: client companies struggle to identify and attract proven leadership talent, especially passive candidates who aren't on job boards.
  2. Ineffective or underdeveloped hiring processes: companies lack structured interview frameworks, consistent assessment criteria, or clear success metrics for new hires. They're winging it and making costly mis-hires.
  3. No internal recruiting resources: mid-sized companies that don't have a dedicated talent acquisition function and rely on job postings, word of mouth, or generalist HR staff to fill executive roles.
  4. Prolonged vacancies: critical leadership roles sitting open for months, creating operational drag and strategic risk.
  5. Mis-hires and regret: the "would you enthusiastically rehire?" question points to clients who have made bad hires and want a better approach.

- **How they describe their process:**
  1. Partner with the client's hiring manager to understand what a successful hire must accomplish (not just the job description)
  2. Develop a position profile outlining first-year expectations and measurables using SMART goals
  3. Conduct direct search to identify passive candidates who have already demonstrated the required outcomes
  4. Rigorously qualify candidates against performance-based criteria (leadership capabilities, career motivations, alignment with business objectives)
  5. Present a small number of highly qualified candidates (often just one)
  6. Support through the interview and offer process
  7. Also offer a Complimentary Hiring Process Effectiveness Review as a door-opener: they look at how the organization defines hiring success, evaluates candidates, and makes hiring decisions, then identify gaps and share practical recommendations

- **Existing messaging samples:**

It's about identifying people who deliver measurable business results. ## SECTION 4: TONE AND VOICE

- **Overall tone:** Consultative, friendly/casual (self-selected on ICP form). Based on the sample messaging, the actual execution leans more consultative and professional than truly casual. It is warm and approachable but not slangy or jokey. Think: experienced advisor who speaks plainly, not corporate, not overly formal, but substantive.

- **Communication style:** They lead with a provocative question ("how many would you enthusiastically rehire?") to create reflection, then build credibility with methodology and results. They are detailed rather than punchy. They tell a story of how they work rather than making bold claims. They are confident but understated: they let the numbers speak (90% ratio, single-candidate hires, 85% repeat business). They do not use aggressive or salesy language.

- **Specific language patterns:**
  - "Enthusiastically rehire" (signature phrase, used repeatedly)

  - "Minimize unnecessary interviews"
  - "Costly mistakes"
  - "Single-candidate presentations"
  - "First-year expectations and measurables"
  - "SMART goals"
  - "Successfully performing for another organization" (how they describe passive candidates)
  - "Complimentary Hiring Process Effectiveness Review"
  - "I'd welcome an opportunity to learn about your organization's..."

- **What to avoid in their voice:**
  - No aggressive sales language ("guaranteed results," "crush the competition," "blow up your pipeline")
  - No staffing industry jargon ("talent acquisition solutions," "end-to-end hiring," "full lifecycle recruiting," "staffing partner")

  - Do not use the word "staffing." They are executive recruiters, not a staffing agency.
  - Do not make it sound transactional. Their messaging is relationship-oriented and consultative.

- **Pronoun usage:** They use "we" (firm-oriented, team feel: "At GRN Piedmont, we believe..." / "Our performance-based recruiting approach..."). They also shift to "I" in the closing ("I'd welcome an opportunity to learn..."). The outreach should use "we" for the firm's methodology and results, and "I" for the personal ask at the end. They reference the company by name: "At GRN Piedmont."
