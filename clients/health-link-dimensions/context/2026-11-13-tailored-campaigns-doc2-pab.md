[Title] HealthLink Dimensions Tailored Campaign Plan, Doc 2
[Subtitle] J&J Innovative Medicine | Large HCP-Facing Device Company | PTC Therapeutics
[Heading1] Why a tailored approach
[FirstParagraph] Doc 1 builds campaigns by segment. Every company in a segment gets the same trigger (an open role) and the same opening line. That works for volume, but the largest and most strategic accounts behave differently. Their HCP data needs change when a brand adds an indication, a device enters a new specialty, or a rare disease company pushes past its first prescribers. Those moments are specific to each account, and the message should be too.
[BodyText] This plan builds three account-level campaigns. Each one keeps the Doc 1 structure (ICP, persona, lead list, messaging) so the two docs can be compared side by side, but the signal, the audience and the reason to talk are unique to the account.
[BodyText] Across all three campaigns:
[Compact] Geography: United States.
[Compact] Offer: a free review of the prospect’s HCP universe for one named brand or product. We compare a sample of their list against ours and show what is out of date, missing or inconsistent, and which new specialists they are not yet reaching. For larger accounts, the review can be scoped as a short paid or unpaid pilot on one brand.
[Compact] Goal: a qualified discovery call.
[Compact] Proof points: monthly-verified provider emails covering 98% of U.S. MDs, DOs, NPs and PAs; 3.8M+ privacy-safe provider emails; 95% client retention and an NPS of 79; TrustArc TRUSTe certified, NAI member, SOC 2; work with 11 of the 15 largest pharma companies.
[Compact] Before launch: confirm none of the three is a current client or on the do-not-contact list. If J&J or the device company already licenses HLD data at the enterprise level, reframe the campaign as an expansion into the brand teams named below.
[Heading1] Campaign A: J&J Innovative Medicine, New Indications Create New Prescriber Universes
[Heading2] ICP
[FirstParagraph] Market: Life sciences, large pharma.
[BodyText] Account: Johnson & Johnson Innovative Medicine (U.S.). We go in through individual brand and data teams, never the enterprise.
[BodyText] Why this account is different: J&J is expanding several brands into specialties those brands have never promoted to. Each expansion means a brand team needs an HCP universe it does not have today:
[Compact] ICOTYDE (icotrokinra): approved in March 2026 for plaque psoriasis (dermatology), with Phase 3 psoriatic arthritis data presented at ACR in 2026. A PsA indication would add rheumatology.
[Compact] RYBREVANT FASPRO: a recent accelerated approval in second-line recurrent or metastatic head and neck cancer moves the brand beyond thoracic oncology to head and neck oncologists.
[Compact] IMAAVY (nipocalimab): approved for generalized myasthenia gravis (neurology) and under review for warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (hematology), with Sjögren’s disease in Phase 3 (rheumatology).
[Compact] CAPLYTA: added through the Intra-Cellular acquisition, prescribed in psychiatry, where NPs and PAs are part of the prescriber base. Our NP and PA coverage is a natural angle here.
[FirstParagraph] Signal (any of these, last 60 days):
[Compact] A new FDA approval, accepted filing or label expansion for a J&J brand.
[Compact] A U.S. job posting on that brand for Omnichannel, Customer Engagement, Commercial Data and Analytics, HCP Targeting or Brand Manager.
[Compact] A new brand lead or omnichannel leader named on that brand.
[FirstParagraph] Size: one account, about 4 to 6 brand teams in scope at a time.
[Heading2] Persona
[Compact] Brand Directors and Senior Brand Managers for ICOTYDE, RYBREVANT, IMAAVY and CAPLYTA.
[Compact] Directors and Senior Directors of Omnichannel or Customer Engagement in Immunology, Oncology and Neuroscience.
[Compact] Commercial Data and Analytics leads who own HCP targeting and third-party data sourcing.
[Compact] Secondary: Strategic Sourcing or Procurement for data vendors, once a brand shows interest.
[Heading2] Lead list
[FirstParagraph] Source: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, filtered to J&J Innovative Medicine U.S. and the therapeutic areas above, cross-checked against recent press releases and job posts.
[BodyText] Who we reach: the brand lead first, then their omnichannel partner and the analytics lead supporting that brand.
[BodyText] Volume: 3 contacts per brand team, 12 to 18 contacts total. Each brand gets its own variant of Message 1.
[BodyText] Filtered out: global roles with no U.S. remit, R&D, Medical Affairs, and anyone in Kenvue or MedTech.

[Heading1] Campaign B: Large HCP-Facing Medical Device Company, Launch and Territory Signals
[Heading2] ICP
[FirstParagraph] Market: Medical devices.
[BodyText] Segment: U.S. medical device companies with 10,000+ employees whose reps and marketers sell directly to surgeons, interventionalists and specialists. Examples: Medtronic, Stryker, Boston Scientific, BD, Edwards Lifesciences, Intuitive and J&J MedTech.
[BodyText] Why this segment is different from Doc 1, Campaign 3: at this size, the HCP data problem is not one CRM. It is many business units, each with its own field force, its own specialty audience and often its own data vendor. A surgeon who uses products from three divisions can exist as three different records. A clean, monthly-verified provider record gives each division the same version of the truth.
[BodyText] Signal (any of these, last 60 days):
[Compact] A new FDA clearance or approval, or a U.S. launch, in a business unit (for example, a new pulsed field ablation system or a surgical robotics platform).
[Compact] A sales force expansion or territory realignment announced in earnings or press.
[Compact] A U.S. job posting for Marketing Operations, CRM, Commercial Excellence, Sales Operations, Professional Education or Digital Marketing in a named business unit.
[Compact] A CRM or marketing automation migration visible in job descriptions.
[FirstParagraph] Size: about 15 to 20 companies, each with 3 to 8 business units worth targeting.
[Heading2] Persona
[Compact] Directors and VPs of Marketing in a named business unit (for example, Electrophysiology, Spine, Structural Heart, Robotics).
[Compact] Commercial Excellence, Sales Operations and CRM leaders.
[Compact] Heads of Professional Education, who run surgeon training and peer-to-peer programs and need accurate surgeon contact data.
[Compact] Marketing Operations and Digital Marketing leads running HCP email programs.
[Heading2] Lead list
[FirstParagraph] Source: Sales Navigator by business unit, matched to recent FDA clearances, earnings call mentions and job posts.
[BodyText] Who we reach: the business unit marketing lead first, then commercial excellence or sales ops, then professional education.
[BodyText] Volume: 3 to 4 contacts per business unit, 2 to 3 business units per company to start.
[BodyText] Filtered out: distributors, consumer health divisions, diagnostics and lab businesses with no specialist promotion, and corporate roles with no business unit tie.

[Heading1] Campaign C: PTC Therapeutics, Moving Past the Centers of Excellence
[Heading2] ICP
[FirstParagraph] Market: Life sciences, commercial-stage rare disease.
[BodyText] Account: PTC Therapeutics (U.S.).
[BodyText] Why this account is different: PTC is not a first launch and not a large pharma. It is a rare disease company whose growth now depends on finding prescribers beyond its core group:
[Compact] SEPHIENCE (sepiapterin) for PKU: all U.S. PKU centers of excellence are now prescribing, along with centers outside that group. PTC has said the next step is going deeper into those centers, and it sizes the U.S. opportunity at about 17,000 patients. Reaching the rest of that population means finding clinicians outside the center network, including adult patients who are no longer in specialty care.
[Compact] Fabry disease gene therapy: PTC acquired a BLA-stage Fabry gene therapy in 2026. That adds nephrologists, cardiologists and geneticists, an audience PTC does not promote to today.
[Compact] KEBILIDI and EMFLAZA: ultra-rare and neuromuscular audiences (pediatric neurologists, neurosurgeons, neuromuscular specialists) where every missing record is a meaningful share of the market.
[FirstParagraph] Signal (any of these, last 60 days):

[Compact] Fabry program regulatory milestones (BLA filing, acceptance, approval).

[FirstParagraph] Size: one account, 3 programs in scope.

[Compact] SEPHIENCE Brand Director and Marketing leads.
[Compact] Omnichannel or Customer Engagement lead.

[Heading1] Notes before launch
[Compact] Confirm client status. Check J&J (both divisions) and the device targets against current HLD clients and the do-not-contact list.
[Compact] Verify the hook on send day. Each campaign leans on a recent event. Confirm the approval, launch or filing is still current the week the sequence goes out, and replace placeholders with the exact brand and specialty.
