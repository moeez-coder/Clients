## Findings that shape the doc

- **The MPC pools are small and rolling.** No single segment has more than about 250 companies with a matching open role in any 90-day window.
  - The MPC campaigns (1 to 5) therefore follow the inverse rule: 3 to 4 contacts per company for segments 1 to 4, and 2 for agencies, which have a larger and more fragmented pool.
  - The base TAMs (553 to 5,508 after cleanup) are large enough for the trigger campaigns (6 to 10) to run on a rolling basis.
- **Industry filters pull in noise.** In the Clay segment 1 sample, only 9 of 20 fit.
  - Noise: CROs, CSOs, research-tools companies (10x, Twist, Revvity, Beckman) and a nonprofit (USP).
  - The sourcing step needs to exclude CRO, CDMO, research tools and staffing by keywords, and run a qualifier prompt.
- **The sample hit 3 existing HLD clients:** EVERSANA, Incyte, and Allergan Aesthetics (AbbVie). The DNC list in tracking_clients is empty (0), so the client's existing-customer list is needed before any pull. The brief's named clients are the minimum exclusion list.
- **Segment 2's 22,169 is not a real TAM.**
  - Small "Biotechnology Research" companies include preclinical startups, CROs and staffing firms. Blitz's sample was Redbock, a staffing company.
  - The real segment is commercial-stage or near-launch companies: Phase 3, a PDUFA date, or a first approval within about 18 months.
  - That has to come from an FDA/pipeline signal, not firmographics. Realistic size: a few hundred companies, cross-checked against the FDA novel approvals and PDUFA calendars.
- **Blitz counts are indicative** (CLAUDE.md accuracy warning). Eyeball the companies at pull time.
