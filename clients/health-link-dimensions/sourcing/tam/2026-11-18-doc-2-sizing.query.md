## 5. Contacts per company (inverse TAM rule) and expected leads

How the rule was applied:
- Each pharma persona's reachable universe is mid-sized (186 to 441 companies), but people are concentrated in about 20 large pharma companies.
- The doc's signal (a role posted in the last 30 days, or an event in the last 60 days) shrinks each month's pool to 35 to 67 companies. That is a small pool, so depth goes up.
- So depth is set by **pool size per persona** (smaller pool, more contacts), with a higher cap at companies that have 50+ persona people, where you can split by franchise or therapy area.

Expected leads = the sum, over companies, of min(cap, persona people at that company). The source is the `matched_people` counts per company, before title-fit cleanup (about 70% to 85% survive).

## 13. Uncertain or noisy

- Blitz `job_level` buckets are its own. "Associate Director" can land in Director, so Decision Makers are probably overcounted and Advocates undercounted.
- Persona people include global roles at US-headquartered companies. A US-remit check is needed at pull time.
- The pharma fit rate comes from 50 sampled companies. The 1,350 "real" figure is ±25%.
- The agency health filter is a keyword test on the LinkedIn about text. It lets through a few general agencies that mention health (Harte Hanks, Jack Morton) and may drop health agencies with thin descriptions.
- Campaign 6's 2 to 4 a month comes from 1 fully classified month plus spot checks. Re-check it monthly.
- Clearances are not filtered by employee count (openFDA has no size field), so the 45 to 60 a month is an estimate.
- The `matched_people` column in the earlier session's `persona_companies.py` output was empty, because it read the wrong response key. Its company counts are unaffected.
