# We Are DNA Campaign Plan, Doc 1

This plan covers ten LinkedIn campaigns, built from your ICP form.

Campaigns 1 to 3 lead the plan. They share one list: about 1,400 UK employers in your five sectors whose own staff rate them below average on public review sites. Each campaign speaks to a different person at the same company, the CEO, the people leader and the talent lead, with a different promise, so we learn which one books the most calls.

Campaigns 4 to 8 are sector campaigns, one per sector. Each one starts from a board-level event that is already public, such as an acquisition programme, a results statement that names people costs, or a gender pay gap report. Events like these usually point to an attraction and retention problem, and that is exactly what your EVP and Employer Brand work fixes. We speak to the CEO and the most senior people leaders, we never lead with job adverts, and we never offer candidates.

Campaigns 9 and 10 reach the same five sectors through a different buying signal from your ICP form:

- new private equity investment
- a new CEO or people leader

Across every campaign:

- **Geography:** United Kingdom, companies headquartered in the UK.
- **Company size:** 201+ employees as the core, with 51 to 200 included where the sector fits.
- **Offer:** Campaigns 1 to 3 start with a free reputation snapshot (Offer 8), which includes what AI tools tell candidates about the company. Campaigns 4 to 10 use a free AI visibility audit as the starting offer. We show the prospect what ChatGPT, Google's AI answers and similar tools tell candidates about them as an employer, and where that differs from the story they want to tell. Eight other offers are set out at the end of this doc for your thoughts, and we can swap any of them in.
- **Goal:** a qualified discovery call with your team.
- **Proof points used:**
  - MAN Truck & Bus UK, £1m a year saved, with attrition cut from 20% to under 10% and agency spend from over £600k to under £30k within a year
  - DHU Healthcare, over £2m added to the bottom line in 20 months
  - Glendola, unwanted staff turnover cut by over 50%
  - boohoo and Pets at Home, over £600k a year saved each
  - Superdry, over £500k a year saved
  - 100+ clients since 2010, NPS of 88
- **Excluded from every list:** your current clients and anything on your do-not-contact list. Your ICP form mentions a do-not-contact file, but it has not reached our system, so please send it again. We also need your current client list (see "Before launch" at the end).

## Campaign 1: Employer Reputation, the Cost of Turnover, CEOs and Managing Directors

### ICP

- **Market:** All five sectors: health, care and veterinary; retail and hospitality; manufacturing and engineering; law and accounting; financial services.
- **Segment:** private-sector UK employers with 51 to 5,000 staff.
- **Signal:** staff rate the company noticeably below average on public employer review sites, on at least 30 reviews. That means an overall rating under 3.5, or culture and values or senior leadership under 3.3. Campaigns 1 to 3 share this list, and each speaks to a different person at the same company.
- **Size:** about 1,400 companies.

### Persona

CEO or Managing Director, and the Chief Operating Officer or Chief Financial Officer at larger groups. You told us you sell to the C-suite, so this campaign talks about money rather than people metrics.

### Signal and data

- **Signal:** public employer review ratings from the last 12 months, at least 30 reviews per company, checked in November 2026.
- **Data:** we checked 29,489 UK employers in your five sectors. 1,796 fall below the bar, and we are confident of the match for 1,384 of them. The rest are held back until we have checked each one by hand.
  - **By sector:** retail and hospitality 560, financial services 329, manufacturing and engineering 191, health, care and veterinary 173, law and accounting 131.
  - **By size:** 51 to 200 staff 389, 201 to 500 staff 375, 501 to 1,000 staff 250, 1,001 to 5,000 staff 370.
- **What the list looks like:** a typical company on it is rated 3.2 overall and 2.8 for senior leadership, and fewer than half of its staff would recommend it to a friend. Senior leadership is the weakest area at almost half of them.
