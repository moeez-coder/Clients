## Files

- `2026-11-17-test-company-lookup-raw.json` / `.csv`: 12 Glassdoor employers returned for 5 names. One per name is `selected` (UK HQ, most reviews); the other 7 are US/Australian lookalikes, a 5–6-review namesake, or a subsidiary.
- `2026-11-17-test-reviews-raw.json` / `.csv`: 100 reviews. **The CSV leaves out job title on purpose** (we never identify reviewers); the raw JSON keeps the full record, as earlier pulls kept raw responses. Sub-ratings of `0` mean "not rated" and are written as `-`.
- `2026-11-17-test-company-scores.csv` (from `test_scores.py`, stdlib, reproducible): per company, Glassdoor overall rating and count, averages of the pulled reviews and six sub-ratings, share of reviews at ≤2 stars, share from UK locations, employer reply rate (all and on ≤2-star reviews), top 3 complaint themes by keyword match on `cons`.

Findings that shape the full run:
1. **Name search works, but it needs a selection rule.** Take the candidate with a UK HQ and the most reviews; reject the rest. Website-domain checks help but Blitz domains are noisy (`harrodscareers.com`), so name + UK HQ + review count is the main rule, with a confidence flag.
2. **Compact mode has no sub-ratings.** For the full run use non-compact with `includeRatings: true` and every other section off (same $0.0002 per company). That also gives the company-level sub-ratings, so most screening can happen **before** paying for reviews.
3. **20 recent reviews cover anything from 6 weeks (Harrods) to 14 months (Shoosmiths).** A "recent decline" test needs a 12-month window, so the full run should pull more per company (e.g. up to 100) and filter by date afterwards. The reviews actor has no date filter.
4. **Reviews are global.** PZ Cussons' recent reviews are only 64% UK (Nigeria, Ghana, Indonesia). Score on UK-located reviews where there are enough.
5. **Employer replies vary sharply** (Coventry replies to 95%, three of five reply to none), so "no replies to negative reviews" is a usable, distinct signal.
6. **The pool has duplicate entities** (e.g. "Shoosmiths - Serious Injury", LinkedIn showcase pages). Dedupe by domain/Glassdoor employer ID before scraping reviews.

# Preview 2: stage-2 lookup at full-run settings, 25 companies (2026-11-17)

Moe's standing rule: preview every new process at its real settings before the full run. This one previews the exact lookup planned for the 4,677-company 501+ slice.

- **Sample:** 25 random companies (`random.seed(17)`) from the 501+ private-sector ICP rows of `2026-11-17-companies.csv`, after deduping by real domain and dropping LinkedIn showcase/school pages (5,210 → 4,677).

- **Files:** `2026-11-17-preview-lookup-raw.json` (29 objects), `2026-11-17-preview-lookup.csv` (25 rows, one per query), `select_and_score.py` (selection rule + profile bar).

So Moe's rules (a: overall < 3.5) and (b: culture or leadership < 3.3), each with 30+ reviews, are judged from the profile alone. Only the **decline** rule needs reviews.

## Selection rule v2 (changed after this preview)

v1 let a domain match beat review count, so "GAIL's" picked a 2-review "Gail's Ltd" shell over the real GAIL's Limited (860 reviews, 3.2). v2: a candidate qualifies by domain match OR shared name tokens; UK HQ first, then the **most reviews** wins. Confidence: high = UK HQ + (domain or strong name match) + 30+ reviews; medium = the same under 30 reviews; low = otherwise (e.g. Denmark-HQ European LifeCare, a Calgary "TEMPS Services", a New York bakery for "Almondine"); none = no candidate.
